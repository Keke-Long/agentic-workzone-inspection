3. Experimental Design and Results
This chapter reports the completed experiments on Work Zone 01. The evaluation separates regulatory retrieval and requirement construction from evidence acquisition and final reporting, following the four-stage organization in Section 2.
3.1 Data Source and Evaluation Reference
The data collection campaign used the laboratory's Level 3 automated driving vehicle at two work zones in Madison, Wisconsin. The results reported here concern the completed reference case, Work Zone 01 (scene-CATS-01); the second site is not included in the quantitative results. Camera images, LiDAR data, and calibration and pose metadata are organized in a nuScenes-style format. This describes the data structure rather than use of the public nuScenes benchmark.
Work Zone 01 contains a single left-lane closure on a four-lane undivided roadway, with a merging taper and an arrow board. The recorded inspection period is daytime, and the posted-speed input is 30 mph, supplied as a scene fact. The selected layout is TA-30. The materialized scene contains 3,631 accepted bounding-box observations across six camera views; these are observations across frames, not 3,631 distinct devices. The current evaluation uses reviewed observations and confirmed device-condition findings, so it evaluates the inspection workflow conditional on those inputs rather than an unassisted perception system.
Requirement selection is scored against a frozen, evaluator-only reference containing 15 applicable items: five mandatory provisions and ten guidance provisions. It covers the six inspection domains defined in Section 2. The reference was produced through a separate autonomous MUTCD content audit and is not expert-validated ground truth or an exhaustive requirement catalog. Its identifiers, target count, and expected answers are withheld from the inspection agent. Candidate recall is the fraction of reference items represented in the retrieved pool; requirement recall is the fraction matched by registered requirements; activation precision is the number of matched items divided by the number of registered requirements.
3.2 Compared Methods and Experimental Controls
Five regulatory-access configurations are evaluated: KG only, BM25 only, Dense only, KG + BM25, and KG + Dense. KG only uses the TA-core and scene-condition graph layers. The retrieval-only baselines use lexical or dense retrieval without these graph layers. The combined methods supplement the graph-derived candidates with BM25 or E5-large-v2 retrieval, respectively. KG + BM25 is the primary method, and KG + Dense tests the alternative supplementary retriever.
The recorded runs use the same scene context, selected TA, inspection domains, source index, evaluator reference, and controller model, gpt-5.6-luna with medium reasoning effort. Supplementary and retrieval-only searches retain up to 18 candidates per domain. Candidate recall is the common comparison across all five methods. Requirement construction is compared directly between the two combined methods, whose retained runs include the structured candidate-decision audit. The retained KG-only run predates that audit and has seven unresolved high-priority candidate dispositions; its downstream scores are historical diagnostics, not a matched ablation of the current compiler. Retrieval-only runs also use a different, free-form registration procedure.
Each configuration has one retained run on this case. The results therefore describe observed behavior without estimating run-to-run variance or statistical significance. The original software's retrieval and requirement-registration stages are both part of Stage 2 in the four-stage framework used in this paper.
3.3 Regulatory Retrieval and Requirement Construction Results
Table 1 summarizes the retained evaluations. All three KG configurations surfaced the complete 15-item reference set. BM25 only retrieved candidates covering two items (13.3%), and Dense only covered four (26.7%). Thus, the graph structure supplied substantially greater candidate coverage in this case. Because KG only already reached full candidate recall, the experiment does not demonstrate an additional recall benefit from supplementary retrieval.
Table 1. Candidate retrieval and registered-requirement results on Work Zone 01.

Both combined methods registered 13 of the 15 reference requirements, giving 86.7% requirement recall. KG + BM25 registered 19 items, with 68.4% activation precision; KG + Dense registered 21, with 61.9% precision. Both matched four of five mandatory items and nine of ten guidance items. Their two missed items concerned advance placement of a Lane Closed warning sign and functional adequacy of the merging taper. These provisions were available in the candidate pool, locating the omissions in requirement construction rather than retrieval.
The unmatched registered items numbered six for KG + BM25 and eight for KG + Dense; each run also contained one explicitly flagged invalid activation. Unmatched items should not all be interpreted as proven regulatory errors because the reference is bounded and not exhaustive. Source-integrity checks passed for every registered item in both combined runs, showing that valid source attribution alone does not establish correct applicability. The retained KG-only and retrieval-only downstream values in Table 1 are diagnostic because their compilation procedures differ from the audited combined runs.
3.4 Evidence Acquisition and System-Level Results
The KG + BM25 pipeline generated availability records for all 19 registered requirements. All records consistently partitioned required inputs and included an evidence strategy and decision rule, but only 15 of 19 plans covered all required acquisition-tool families. The planning audit therefore did not fully pass. During the evidence-acquisition run, 36 evidence records accounted for all 19 requirements, including four acquisition-failure records. All 23 inventoried traffic-sign representatives and the single arrow-board representative were inspected, and each of the four inventoried channelizer classes was sampled. These are coverage measures over the accepted inventory, not detection accuracy or proof that every field device was inspected.
The original sealed pipeline produced 14 satisfied, two deficient, and three not_assessed outcomes. Two pending geometric checks were subsequently completed in a documented evaluation supplement, without changing the normative requirement registry. The resulting report contains 16 satisfied, two deficient, and one not_assessed outcome (Table 2). Assessment coverage increased from 16/19 (84.2%) to 18/19 (94.7%); these percentages measure the availability of a definitive status, not judgment accuracy or overall work-zone compliance.
Table 2. Recorded assessment outcomes before and after supplementary geometry evaluation.

The two deficient requirements refer to MUTCD Section 6K.01, paragraphs 14 and 15, and share one consolidated channelizing-device-condition issue. The supporting findings identify substantial retroreflective-band loss on device I4844 and damaged or peeling bands on I5502. These findings were supplied through the reviewed scene evidence and are not counted as independent model detections. The report preserves both requirement-level conclusions while grouping their common physical evidence.
Geometric evaluation covered 30 channelizer gaps, with no reliable clear exceedance reported under the registered thresholds. Supplementary sign-spacing estimates for A, B, and C were 270.8, 249.8, and 219.4 ft, respectively, exceeding the registered 100-ft recommendation. The fitted taper length was 158.7 ft, compared with a registered minimum of 140.9 ft using a lateral offset of 9.39 ft and a speed of 30 mph. These track-derived values are screening measurements; no independent survey-based error estimate is available. The remaining not_assessed item concerns the highway-conditions warning-sign provision in Section 6H.02, paragraph 1.

3.5 Computational Cost
Table 3 reports resource use for retrieval and requirement construction in the two audited combined runs; it excludes downstream inspection, data preparation, and supplementary geometry evaluation. KG + Dense used approximately 1.88 times the tokens and 1.30 times the wall time of KG + BM25 while achieving the same requirement recall and lower activation precision. This case supports BM25 as the less expensive supplementary retriever under the recorded settings, but does not establish a general ranking across work zones.

Costs are the model-API estimates stored with the runs under their checkpoint pricing, not current service quotations or total deployment costs. Wall times are single-run observations and include neither uncertainty estimates nor a hardware-normalized benchmark.
3.6 Limitations of the Current Evaluation
The completed evidence supports a single-case feasibility study. Generalization across work zones, repeatability across model runs, and final judgment accuracy remain unestablished. Reviewed bounding boxes and confirmed condition findings contribute to the current input, and the requirement reference has not undergone independent expert validation. Object-detection mAP, condition-classification accuracy, survey-based geometry errors, and human report-quality scores are therefore not reported.
The final status counts also require caution: several retained rationales assign satisfied because no clear violation was established while acknowledging unresolved visual details. This falls short of the positive-evidence criterion described in Section 2 and should be assessed through an independent conclusion audit. Reporting these outcomes as system outputs, separately from requirement-retrieval scores and the later geometry supplement, prevents procedural completeness from being mistaken for validated inspection accuracy.
